  • use the words and chunks of 4 groups: video games, verb + preposition, communication, history;

  • choose say or tell correctly and avoid said me or told to me;

  • be curious about history and about how people communicated in the past;

Grammar & sentence patterns

Grammar 1 · Reported speech — what people said

Five rules
💬DIRECT OR REPORTED?
  • direct speech = the exact words, in quotation marks
  • reported speech = the idea of what someone said, in your own sentence — no quotation marks
  • that after said / told is optional
  • Mike said, 'I'll see you tomorrow.'
  • Mike said (that) he'd see me the next day.
⏪THE TENSE MOVES ONE STEP BACK
  • present simple → past simple · present continuous → past continuous
  • present perfect → past perfect · past simple → past perfect (or past simple if the time is clear)
  • will → would · can → could · may → might · must → had to
  • 'We love it.' → People said they loved it.
  • 'We are working on a new device.' → Nintendo said they were working on a new device.
  • 'We've never seen anything like it.' → People said they had never seen anything like it.
  • 'We will produce games for families.' → They said they would produce games for families.
👤PRONOUNS AND POSSESSIVES CHANGE
  • I → he / she · we → they · me → him / her
  • my → his / her · our → their
  • they change to fit the person who reports (your own words keep I / my)
  • 'I need the TV for my game.' → Jack said he needed the TV for his game.
  • 'We live here.' → They said they lived there.
📅TIME AND PLACE WORDS CHANGE
  • now → then · today → that day · this → that · here → there
  • tomorrow → the next day / the following day
  • yesterday → the day before / the previous day · ago → before
  • they stay the same if the time or place is still the same when you report
  • 'I'll call you tomorrow.' → She said she would call me the next day.
  • 'I bought it two years ago.' → He said he had bought it two years before.
✋WHEN THE TENSE DOESN'T CHANGE
  • the reporting verb is in the present: says · has said
  • a fact or habit that is still true (the change is optional)
  • could · would · should · might stay the same
  • The forecast says it will rain tomorrow.
  • Our teacher said that water boils at 100°C.
  • He said we should visit the museum.

Grammar 2 · Reporting verbs: say and tell

Four rules
🗣️SAY + (THAT) + WHAT WAS SAID
  • say has no person object after it
  • to name the listener: say (something) to someone — but tell + person is more common
  • The message said that his boat was sinking.
  • He said he was lost. · She said goodbye to us.
👂TELL + PERSON + (THAT) + WHAT WAS SAID
  • tell always has a person object: tell me / him / them / reporters
  • no to before the person
  • He told news reporters that he had been very curious.
  • Dave told me he would call me back later.

Grammar 3 · Verb + preposition

Four rules
➕VERB + PREPOSITION + NOUN / PRONOUN
  • the preposition belongs to the verb
  • it comes before the person or thing
  • Do you agree with them?
  • How much do you pay for a game?
🔤PREPOSITION + ‑ING
  • after a preposition, use the ‑ing form of a verb
  • We're thinking about visiting Hue.
  • She worries about failing the exam.
❓IN QUESTIONS, IT STAYS AT THE END
  • wh‑ question → the preposition goes after the verb, at the end
  • Who are you talking about?
  • Which team does he play for?
  • What are you waiting for?
🧩LEARN THE VERB WITH ITS PREPOSITION
  • a different preposition can change the meaning
  • check a learner's dictionary when you meet a new verb
  • play with friends · play against a rival · play for a team · play in teams
  • agree with someone · agree on a plan
