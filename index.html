Lead-in 2 · Agree or disagree?

4:00Tap your view, then give one reason and one example.

1 · Video games are a waste of time for medical students.

Model answer

I partly disagree. Some games are a waste of time, but a short puzzle game after a long day on the ward helps me relax. The real problem is losing track of time, so I stop after half an hour.

2 · Handwritten letters mean more than text messages.

Model answer

I agree. In my first year at the academy, my grandmother sent me a letter, and it's still in my drawer. I've deleted hundreds of texts since then, but I still read that letter.

3 · Young people today don't need to learn history.

Model answer

I disagree. History explains why our country is the way it is. Even in medicine we learn from the mistakes doctors made a hundred years ago.

4 · Old buildings should be pulled down to make space for new hospitals and schools.

Model answer

I don't agree at all. A city needs hospitals, but it also needs its memory. If planners think carefully, there's usually space for both.

5 · Every school should make a time capsule for future students.

Model answer

I agree. It's cheap, it's fun, and it makes students think about their own lives. Twenty years later, the letters inside would show how much everything had changed.

6 · Doctors should keep paper notes as well as electronic records.

Model answer

I partly agree. Computers can crash, so a paper copy of the key information is a good backup. But writing everything twice takes too long, so I'd keep paper notes only for emergencies.

Useful language
  • I completely agree / I partly agree / I don't agree at all, because …
  • It depends on …
  • In my experience, …
  • That's true, but …

Transcript

Just over one hundred years ago, the British explorer Captain Robert Falcon Scott died with his team of men in the snow and ice of Antarctica. He had reached the South Pole, but never returned to this hut, which was the starting point for his expedition. Now the hut is falling down under the snow, and we would like to save it – not just because of its connection to Scott, though this of course is important. Actually, we are more interested in what you find inside the hut. Because of the freezing temperatures in this part of the world, the hut has become a time capsule. There are items of food, such as butter, biscuits and tins of meat, which are one hundred years old. The ice has preserved them all. There are even some of Scott's old possessions and equipment, and things like soap and medicine bottles. When you go inside the hut, it's almost as if he has only just left it. I think we need to look after it because it tells us so much about Scott, but also about our own past.

Step 2 The topic sentence

GoalFind, choose and complete a topic sentence with ONE controlling idea.
Why this worksThe topic sentence is the most important sentence; weak, general or announcement-style topic sentences are among the most common problems in Vietnamese learners' paragraphs.
Step 2a

Tap the controlling idea — the part the paragraph will prove

Topic sentence = topic + controlling idea. The controlling idea limits the topic: it tells the reader exactly what the paragraph will prove.

1

Because it was built to look like a lotus flower, the One Pillar Pagoda in Hà Nội is a special historical place.

2

In 1945, the Declaration of Independence was an important event because it marked the birth of a new nation.

3

Captain Scott's hut in Antarctica is important today because it has become a time capsule of early polar exploration.
Why?
  1. is a special historical place is too general on its own: the paragraph will show how it was built to look like a lotus flower.
  2. was an important event is too general on its own: the paragraph will prove that it marked the birth of a new nation.
  3. is important today needs a limit: the paragraph will show how the hut works as a time capsule.
Step 2b

Choose the best topic sentence

Test each option against ALL the supporting sentences. Avoid: a statement that is too general, an announcement (In this paragraph I will …) and a single fact.

Step 3 Unity and logical order

GoalSpot the sentence that does not belong; order sentences with all the clues.
Why this worksUnity and coherence come from key nouns, pronouns, linkers and logical order — not from linkers alone.
Step 3a

Unity: tap the sentence that does not belong

Every sentence must support the controlling idea. Careful: the wrong sentence is true, formal and uses the topic's key words — it just proves something else.

1 · Ô ăn quan

Ô ăn quan is a good game for children because it develops their maths and planning skills. Players have to count the stones in each square before every move. Many adults also remember playing it in their village when they were young. They also need to plan several moves ahead in order to capture the other player's stones. As a result, children practise mental arithmetic and strategic thinking without noticing it.

2 · Hội An

Hội An Ancient Town is a unique historical place because its buildings reflect three different cultures. Many old shophouses were built by Chinese merchants and have Chinese-style roofs. The famous Covered Bridge is often called the Japanese Bridge. Today, the town is also famous for its tailors, who can make a suit in one day. Finally, the wooden houses along the river follow traditional Vietnamese designs. Together, these buildings show how different communities lived and traded there for centuries.

3 · Bạch Đằng

The Battle of Bạch Đằng in 938 was a turning point because it ended more than a thousand years of Chinese rule. Ngô Quyền's army hid sharp wooden stakes in the riverbed. When the tide went out, the enemy ships were caught on the stakes and destroyed. The Bạch Đằng River is also known for its beautiful landscape and fresh seafood. After the victory, Ngô Quyền became king, and a long period of independence began.
Why?
  1. Sentence 3 is about adults' memories, not about maths and planning skills.
  2. Sentence 4 is true and about Hội An, but tailors have nothing to do with buildings that reflect three cultures.
  3. Sentence 4 describes the river today; it does not explain why the battle was a turning point.

Step 4 Linkers by function

GoalChoose a linker for the relation it shows — or decide that no linker is needed.
Why this worksLearners overuse moreover and double linkers as in Vietnamese (although … but, because … so); choosing by function fixes both.
Step 4a

Choose the linker by its function — or no linker

Functions: addition · example · contrast · result · time · conclusion. One gap needs no linker.

Folk games: from village yards to school festivals

Folk games have been part of Vietnamese childhood for centuries. Popular games, (1) ô ăn quan and kéo co, need almost no equipment. (2) many children in cities now spend most of their free time indoors. Some schools have decided to bring these games back. (3) a primary school can organise a tug-of-war contest at its spring festival. Teamwork is essential in these games. (4) children learn to communicate quickly and clearly. Many of them (5) also make new friends in other classes. (6) some parents still worry about small injuries, folk games have clearly become a healthy social activity again.

Why?
  1. example inside the sentence: such as + noun
  2. contrast after a full stop: However,
  3. example after a full stop: For instance, (such as cannot start a sentence)
  4. result: As a result,
  5. no linker: also already adds the idea — moreover … also or in addition … also says the same thing twice
  6. concession inside one sentence: Although + clause, + main clause

2b
Which one, and why?
7′
1 · The nurse ____ the patient that his X-ray was normal.
2 · She ____ that the message was a bit disappointing.
3 · After graduation, let's keep ____ each other.
4 · Sorry, I forgot to reply ____ your email about the meeting.
5 · For years, special glass cases have ____ these old maps and letters.
6 · The new hospital has bought a lot of modern medical ____.
7 · Doctors ran more tests to ____ what was causing her fever.
8 · Archaeologists found some ____ tombs that are over two thousand years old.
Why?
  1. told — a person (the patient) comes straight after it.
  2. said — no person after it; told needs one, and never told to.
  3. in touch with — the chunk needs in before touch and with before the person.
  4. to — reply to a message.
  5. preserved — kept as it was; reserve = book; prevent = stop.
  6. equipment — uncountable: no ‑s and no an.
  7. find out — discover information; find is for things.
  8. ancient — from a time very long ago; old-fashioned = no longer modern.

1The doctor said me that my blood test was normal.
→
tell + a person (told me); say never has a person straight after it.
2The guide told that the temple was over five hundred years old.
→
There is no person after the verb, so use said; told needs a person (told reporters).
3My grandmother still keeps touch with her old army friends.
→
The chunk is keep in touch with + a person.
4If you lose your student card, please keep in touch with the academy office at once.
→
get in touch = make contact (once); keep in touch = stay in contact over a long time.
5Can you reply to the phone, please? My hands are full.
→
You answer the phone, the door or a question; you reply to a message or an email.
6Our field hospital needs more equipments for the winter exercise.
→
equipment is uncountable: no ‑s and no an.
7The library has a whole shelf of historic novels set in old Hà Nội.
→
historical = about the past (a historical novel); historic = very important in history.
8Before our visit, I tried to find more about the museum online.
→
find out (about) = discover information; find = discover a thing.
9After it hit the ice, the old ship slowly began to drown.
→
Boats sink; people and animals drown. After began to, use the base form.
10A week ago, our captain said the room inspection would be tomorrow, so we cleaned all night.
→
Reported a week later, tomorrow becomes the next day.
